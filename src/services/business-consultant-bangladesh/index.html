The problems I'm usually called for

Most owners don't call a consultant because of a strategy question. They call because something that should be simple keeps going wrong, every month, and nobody has time to find out why. If one of these sounds familiar, this page is for you.

Inventory

The stock never matches the system.

Every count finds a difference, store blames production, production blames purchase, and the year-end stock take takes the factory offline for three days. Why it happens.

Costing

Nobody is sure what a product really costs.

Prices are set from an old costing sheet, wastage is a guess, and the orders that look most profitable may be the ones losing money. The five usual mistakes.

Payroll

Payroll takes a week and is still wrong.

Attendance arrives late, overtime is approved on paper, and HR rebuilds the salary sheet in Excel every month. A faster close.

Owner dependency

Everything waits for the owner.

Purchase approvals, price changes, credit to customers: all go to one desk. The business can only grow as fast as one person can read WhatsApp.

Reporting

Monthly numbers arrive late, and nobody trusts them.

The management report is assembled by hand from five spreadsheets, three weeks after month-end, and it starts an argument rather than a decision.

Systems

You've outgrown Excel, or the ERP didn't help.

Either the company runs on spreadsheets that only two people understand, or it bought an ERP that the team works around. Consultant or software?

What a business consultant actually does here

The word "consultant" covers everything from a strategy firm's slide deck to a software salesman. My work is narrower and more practical: I look at how goods, money, people and decisions actually move through your company, find the few places where that flow breaks, and help your team fix them. I stay until the fix is part of how people work, which is where most consulting stops too early.

In practice that means four kinds of work:

  • Diagnose. Interviews and floor walks in each department, a sample of real transactions traced end to end, and spot-checks of stock, costs and payroll against what the records say.
  • Design. Agree the new way of working: who does what, with which document, by when, and who approves. Written as short process maps and one-page SOPs people can follow.
  • Implement. Put it in place with your team: clean the item master, set up a counting routine, rebuild the costing sheet, fix the payroll calendar, and configure the ERP where a system is the right tool.
  • Hand over. Train the people who will own it, agree the three or four numbers that show it is working, and check in monthly until they stay healthy on their own.

How an engagement works

Four steps. Each is small enough to approve on its own, and you can stop after any of them with something useful in hand.

  1. First conversation

    45 minutes · online or in Dhaka · free

    You describe what's going wrong and what has been tried. I tell you honestly whether it's worth a diagnostic, or whether two or three changes you can make yourselves will do. You get a short written note either way.

  2. Operations diagnostic

    1–2 weeks · fixed fee agreed upfront

    A structured look at the areas that matter for your business. It ends with a one-page owner report (red, amber, green, in plain language), the evidence behind each finding, and a prioritised 90-day plan with owners and costs.

  3. Improvement project

    6–16 weeks · fixed scope from the diagnostic

    We fix the top items together: processes redesigned, data cleaned, routines set up, people trained, and the ERP implemented or repaired where it's needed. Weekly progress against the numbers agreed in the diagnostic.

  4. Advisory retainer

    Monthly · fixed hours · optional

    A monthly review of the key numbers, help with new problems as the business grows, and training for new managers. The aim is that you need me less every quarter.

What I don't do

  • Tax, VAT and audit. Those belong with your accountant or a chartered accountancy firm. Where a process touches tax, I work with them so the system produces what they need.
  • Legal and labour-law opinions. I design payroll and HR processes that follow your policy and the rules your HR or legal adviser confirms; I don't interpret the law for you.
  • Reselling software. I'm not paid commission on licences, so I can tell you when you don't need a new system.
  • Reports that sit in a drawer. If an engagement ends with only a document, it hasn't worked.

Who this is for

Mostly owner-led companies with roughly 30 to 2,000 people: garment, textile, light-engineering, plastics, food and other manufacturers, exporters, importers, traders and distributors, based in Dhaka, Gazipur, Narayanganj, Chattogram or elsewhere in Bangladesh. Remote work is possible for most of the diagnostic and design; floor walks and training are best done in person.

Frequently asked questions

What does a business consultant do for a small or mid-sized company in Bangladesh?

A business consultant finds where time and money leak out of the way a company runs, explains why in plain language, and helps fix it. For a Bangladeshi manufacturer or trader that usually means stock that doesn't match the books, product costs that are guesses, payroll that takes a week, purchasing without control, late reports and an owner who must approve everything. A useful consultant stays to put the fixes in place and trains the team, rather than leaving a report.

How is this different from hiring an ERP vendor?

An ERP vendor sells and configures software. A business consultant starts from the problem and decides whether software is part of the answer at all. Many problems, such as unclear responsibilities, missing documents between departments or no counting routine, are fixed with process and discipline. When software is needed, I can implement it, usually Odoo, but I'm not paid to resell licences.

What size of company do you work with?

Mostly owner-led companies with roughly 30 to 2,000 employees, where the owner or managing director is still close to daily decisions. Smaller companies usually benefit most from a short diagnostic and a do-it-yourself plan.

Do you give tax, VAT or audit advice?

No. That stays with your accountant or a chartered accountancy firm. I work on operations and the systems behind them, and coordinate with your accountant where a process touches tax.

How much does a business consultant cost in Bangladesh?
