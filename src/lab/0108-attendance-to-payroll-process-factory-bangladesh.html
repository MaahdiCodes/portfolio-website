Where the Week Goes

Ask a factory HR team to map the days between month-end and salary payment, and the picture is usually similar:

  • Days 1–2: waiting for attendance from devices, sections and outside locations; chasing missing punches.
  • Days 2–3: collecting leave applications and overtime sheets on paper; finding that half need a signature.
  • Days 3–4: updating joiners, leavers, transfers and salary changes that weren't entered during the month.
  • Days 4–5: running payroll, then correcting it in Excel because something didn't come through.
  • Days 5–7: approvals, bank or mobile-wallet files, queries from workers whose pay is wrong.

Very little of that is calculation. It's waiting, chasing and correcting. The fix is to move that work into the month, when the information is fresh, so that by month-end payroll only has to run and be checked.

The Cut-off Calendar

Every department needs to know exactly when its inputs are due, and that late inputs move to next month's payroll rather than holding up everyone else's. A calendar like this works for a monthly payroll (D = the last day of the pay period):

WhenWhatWho
D−5Last date for salary changes, promotions and new bank details for this monthHR, department heads
D−3Joiners and leavers for the month entered; final settlements listedHR
DPeriod closes; attendance devices synced; outside-location attendance receivedHR, IT, site in-charges
D+1Attendance exceptions cleared; leave and overtime approvals locked; attendance signed off by each sectionSection heads, HR
D+2Payroll run; pre-payment checks; variance review; approvalPayroll, finance, management
D+3 onwardsBank or mobile-wallet file sent; payslips issued; queries loggedFinance, HR

The dates matter less than agreeing them, publishing them, and holding to them. Bangladesh's labour law also sets a deadline for paying wages after the wage period ends; check the current rule with your HR or legal adviser and build the calendar so it's met comfortably, not on the last day.

Fix Attendance Daily, Not Monthly

The biggest single improvement is to stop treating attendance as a month-end job. Every morning, HR should get an exceptions list from the previous day:

  • missing in or out punches,
  • people absent without approved leave,
  • people present on a scheduled holiday or weekly off,
  • punches outside the assigned shift,
  • overtime worked without an approval.

Each section head clears their own exceptions that day, while people still remember what happened. A month of exceptions at once, for hundreds or thousands of workers, is what takes the time and produces the errors.

Two other things help a lot: a published shift roster, so the system knows who should be where, and one attendance source of truth. If some workers punch on devices, some sign a register, and some are reported by WhatsApp from a site, payroll will always start with reconciliation.

Overtime: Approve Before, Not After

Overtime is where most payroll disputes and most cost leakage happen. The common pattern is a paper sheet signed by a supervisor at month-end, covering hours that nobody else can verify.

  • Approve in advance. The section head requests overtime for named workers and hours before the shift, with a reason, and a manager approves it.
  • Match to attendance. Pay the lower of approved hours and hours actually recorded on the device. Differences go on the exceptions list.
  • Watch the limits. The law limits working hours and overtime per day and per week. Configure those limits in your system or spreadsheet so anything above them is flagged, and confirm the current limits with your HR or legal adviser.
  • Review the pattern. A section with high overtime every month has a planning or staffing problem, not an overtime problem.

The overtime rate itself is a calculation your system should do the same way every time. The RMG wage calculator shows the common calculation for garment workers, and wage-board payroll in Odoo covers setting it up.

Joiners, Leavers and Changes

Every change to who gets paid what should arrive as a simple form with an effective date and an approval, during the month, not as a list at month-end:

  • Joiners: entered when they join, with grade, salary structure, shift and payment method.
  • Leavers: entered on their last day, with the type of separation, so the final settlement is calculated correctly.
  • Changes: promotions, transfers, salary revisions and deductions, each with an effective date and approver.
  • Bank and wallet details: changed only with the employee's written request, and verified by a call-back to the employee. A changed account number is the most common route for payroll fraud.

Checks Before Anyone Is Paid

A fast payroll is only useful if it's right. Before approval, run the same checks every month:

  1. Headcount reconciliation: last month's headcount + joiners − leavers = this month's paid headcount, by department.
  2. Month-on-month variance: compare each person's net pay with last month and explain every change above, say, 10%.
  3. Totals by department: gross, overtime, deductions and net, compared with last month and budget.
  4. Red flags: people paid with zero attendance, two people with the same bank account or wallet number, negative net pay, overtime above the limits, final settlements without a separation record.
  5. Sign-off: payroll prepares, finance checks, management approves. Never the same person.

The free payroll close calendar & checklist (Excel) has these checks as a ready list, a variance sheet that flags changes over your threshold, and an overtime approval log.

Run the variance check first

If you only add one control, make it the month-on-month variance review. It catches most errors, from a missed attendance file to a wrong salary change, in about an hour, and it's the check auditors and owners understand immediately.

Paying and Closing

Generate the bank or mobile-wallet payment file directly from the approved payroll, never retyped. After payment, reconcile the confirmation from the bank or wallet provider against the payroll, and investigate every failed or returned payment the same day. Issue payslips, in Bangla for workers, and log every query with its cause. Next month, look at the causes: they tell you which input arrived wrong.

Payroll errors are almost never calculation errors. They are input errors that arrived too late to be checked.

Three Numbers to Track

  • Close time: working days from period end to approved payroll.
  • Corrections after approval: how many payments had to be corrected or paid separately.
  • Queries per 100 employees: pay complaints logged after payslips are issued.

When the first falls and the other two fall with it, the process is working. If close time falls but corrections rise, the team is cutting checks to hit the date.

Where a System Helps

Everything above works in Excel for a small workforce. Beyond a few hundred employees, a payroll system pays for itself: attendance flows from devices without re-keying, approvals are recorded, the calculation is the same every month, and payment files come straight from the approved run. HR and payroll in Odoo covers one way to set it up. But a system doesn't fix late inputs; the calendar and the daily exceptions do.

Frequently asked questions

How long should factory payroll take?

With inputs managed during the month, two to three working days from period end to an approved payroll is realistic for most factories, including large ones. If it takes longer, map the days: the delay is almost always in collecting and correcting inputs, not in calculation.

What causes most payroll errors?

Late or incomplete inputs: missing attendance, overtime not matched to approvals, joiners and leavers entered late, and salary changes without effective dates. A month-on-month variance check before approval catches most of them.

Should overtime be approved before or after it's worked?

Before. Approval in advance controls cost and makes overtime part of planning. Pay the lower of approved and recorded hours, and treat any difference as an exception to explain.

Do we need payroll software?

Not for a small team with a good calendar and checks. Above a few hundred employees, or with several sites and shifts, software removes re-keying and makes the calculation consistent, which usually justifies the cost quickly.
