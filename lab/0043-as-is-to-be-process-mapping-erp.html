AS-IS vs TO-BE Process Mapping — a practitioner's guide.

Implementing an ERP system without mapping your current and future processes is like building a house without a blueprint. Here is how Bangladesh's leading manufacturers use AS-IS and TO-BE mapping to guarantee ERP success.

A fatal mistake many companies make during ERP implementation is jumping straight into software configuration. They assume the software will magically fix their operational bottlenecks. But if you digitize a broken process, you just get a faster broken process.

As a Business Analyst specializing in Odoo, my first step with any new client—before we even log into the system—is mapping the AS-IS (current state) and designing the TO-BE (future state). This is the foundation of the Discovery Session.

If you don't know exactly how you work today, you cannot accurately design how you should work tomorrow.

Defining the States

The AS-IS State

The unfiltered, brutally honest reality of how your business operates right now. It includes every manual Excel sheet, every verbal approval on WhatsApp, every shadow IT system, and every bottleneck.

Goal: To understand the pain points and uncover "hidden" steps that employees take to keep the business running.

The TO-BE State

The optimized future state where your operations are supported by the new ERP system. Redundancies are eliminated, manual data entry is automated, and approvals are streamlined.

Goal: To create the blueprint for system configuration and define exactly how the business will operate post go-live.

Why Process Mapping Matters

Why spend weeks mapping processes when you could just start installing modules? Here are the three primary reasons:

  • Preventing Scope Creep: A clear TO-BE map acts as the definitive contract of what will be built. If a feature isn't in the TO-BE map, it is out of scope.
  • Identifying Gaps Early: If your AS-IS process requires a unique 5-level approval for international LCs, and the ERP only natively supports 2 levels, you identify this gap on day one, not during UAT.
  • Change Management: Showing end-users a visual map of how their daily work will change from "AS-IS" to "TO-BE" reduces anxiety and accelerates adoption. We discussed this heavily in BPMN Templates for Manufacturing.

Phase 1: The AS-IS Discovery

Mapping the AS-IS state requires interviewing the actual people doing the work, not just the managers. Managers know how the process is supposed to work; floor workers know how it actually works.

1. Gemba Walk
Physically walk the factory floor or office. Follow a single order from the moment a customer calls to the moment the invoice is paid.
Observation
2. Document Artifacts
Collect every piece of paper, every Excel template, and every register book used in the process. Ask: "Where do you get this data?" and "Who do you give this to?"

3. Draft the Map
Use BPMN tools (like Draw.io or Lucidchart) to visually map the flow. Highlight areas with high manual intervention in red.

Phase 2: Designing the TO-BE

Once the AS-IS is documented and approved by the stakeholders, the Business Analyst puts on their architect hat. The TO-BE state should not just replicate the AS-IS state in Odoo; it should eliminate waste.

Ask these critical questions:

  • Why does this step exist? Is it required for compliance, or is it just "how we've always done it"?
  • Can Odoo automate this step entirely? (e.g., Automatic reordering rules instead of manual stock checks).
  • Can we consolidate approvals to reduce bottlenecks without sacrificing control?

Bridging the Gap: The Gap Analysis Document

The difference between your TO-BE design and the native capabilities of Odoo is your "Gap." The Gap Analysis Document details exactly how you will handle these differences. You have three choices for every gap:

  1. Change the Business (Recommended): Adapt your business process to match Odoo's native workflow. This is cheapest and easiest to maintain.
  2. Workaround: Find a creative way to use native features to achieve the goal without custom code.
  3. Customize: Write custom Python modules to force Odoo to behave exactly like your TO-BE design. (Use sparingly, as it increases maintenance costs).

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between AS-IS and TO-BE process mapping?

AS-IS process mapping documents exactly how a business operates today, including all inefficiencies and manual steps. TO-BE mapping designs the future workflow, showing how the business will operate once the new ERP system is implemented, optimizing out those inefficiencies.

Why is process mapping important for ERP implementation?

Skipping process mapping is the leading cause of ERP failure. Without knowing your AS-IS, you cannot accurately identify the gaps the ERP needs to fill. Without a TO-BE, your implementation team will simply rebuild your old, broken processes in a new, expensive software.

What tools are best for process mapping?

Standard BPMN 2.0 (Business Process Model and Notation) is the industry standard. Tools like Draw.io, Lucidchart, or Microsoft Visio are excellent for creating these visual diagrams. The focus should be on clarity and standard notation.
