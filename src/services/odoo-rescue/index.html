Who calls for a rescue

Rescue work comes in four shapes. If one of these sounds like your company, you are in the right place.

Live, but not used

Odoo is live, and the team still runs on Excel.

Go-live happened. Months later the stock figures don't match the warehouse, accounts re-key everything into spreadsheets, and the owner gets reports nobody believes.

Stalled mid-way

The project stopped, or the partner moved on.

Half the modules are configured, the go-live date has slipped twice, the original consultant has left, and nobody can say what is actually finished.

Built in-house

Your own team built it, and it's getting out of hand.

A small in-house team, often moving fast with AI coding tools, built payroll, inventory and accounts. It worked at first. Now every change breaks something else.

About to go live

Go-live is close, and it doesn't feel ready.

UAT is patchy, the opening balances aren't signed off, and the users haven't really been trained. You want an independent second opinion before you switch over.

Why ERP projects fail. It's rarely the software.

Odoo is mature software running at thousands of companies. When an implementation fails, the cause is almost always in four places, and none of them is the code:

  1. The process was never mapped. The system was configured from a module checklist instead of from how goods, money and approvals actually move through the business. Users meet a system that doesn't fit their job, so they work around it. (More in Why ERP fails in Bangladesh.)
  2. The data went in dirty. Duplicate products, wrong units of measure, unreconciled opening balances. Every report built on that data is wrong, and people stop trusting the system within weeks. (See the data migration checklist.)
  3. Customisation replaced decisions. Every "we've always done it this way" became a custom module. Now upgrades are blocked and nobody remembers why half of them exist.
  4. Training was a demo, not a change. Users watched a walkthrough a week before go-live and were then left alone. Adoption is a management job, not a training slot. (See Change management isn't a memo.)

The industry numbers say this is normal, not bad luck. Panorama Consulting's annual ERP surveys have repeatedly found that around half or more of organisations suffer a material operational disruption at go-live: 51% in 2014 and 56% in 2017, such as being unable to ship goods or close the books (Panorama 2017 ERP Report). The implementer has usually moved on to the next sale by the time the disruption bites. Rescue starts there.

The point of a rescue

AI tools have made software cheaper to build. They haven't made process design, data cleanup, user training or change management any cheaper, and those cause most failures. Whatever you built, with a partner, in-house or with AI, the job is to make sure your people use it correctly and your numbers are right.

How a rescue works

Four steps. Each one is small enough to approve on its own, and you can stop after any of them with something useful in hand.

  1. Free health check

    45 minutes · online or in person · free

    One conversation with the owner or project lead about what is going wrong and what has been tried. You get one written page of first findings and an honest view of whether a rescue is worth it. Sometimes the answer is "you don't need me, fix these three things".

  2. System audit

    1–2 weeks · fixed fee agreed upfront

    A structured review of processes, data, configuration, customisations, access rights, compliance and user behaviour, delivered as a one-page red–amber–green report for the owner plus a detailed findings log for the team. It ends with a prioritised fix list and a rescue-or-restart recommendation.

  3. Stabilise & fix sprint

    4–8 weeks · fixed scope from the audit

    Repair the issues that matter most: clean and re-own master data, redesign the broken flows, reconcile stock and opening balances, rebuild access rights, retrain users role by role, and bring Mushak and VAT reporting back into the system. Where the fix is code, I write the specification and test the result with your developers or a vetted technical partner.

  4. Adoption & care retainer

    Monthly · fixed hours

    Keeps the system healthy after the sprint: a set number of support hours, refresher and new-joiner training, a monthly system-health report, and updates whenever NBR changes a VAT rule or the wage board changes payroll. The goal is that your own super-users need me less every quarter.

Rescue or restart?

The most expensive mistake in a failing project is choosing the wrong path. A full re-implementation throws away working configuration, data and everything your users have already learned. A rescue that ignores a broken foundation just delays the restart. The audit decides it on evidence, roughly like this:

Usually rescueUsually restart
Data is messy but recoverable with cleanup and re-importThe database is inconsistent at the accounting level and can't be reconciled
Problems sit in configuration, process and trainingCore Odoo code was modified, and every upgrade is blocked
Users dislike the system but use parts of it dailyThe edition or version is wrong for the business (for example, missing modules the business depends on)
Customisations are few or replaceable by standard featuresThe system has never been used for real transactions at all

If you are changing implementation partners as part of this, read Switching Odoo partners mid-implementation first. It covers handover, code ownership and contract exit.

Built it yourselves? That's fine too.

More companies are skipping ERP vendors and building in-house, often with a small team using AI coding tools. That can work. It usually gets hard at the same point: once payroll, inventory, production, accounts and VAT all live in one system, every change has side effects, and the code grows faster than anyone's understanding of the business rules inside it.

I don't compete with your developers. I work as their functional architect and QA partner: I map the processes the system has to support, define the business rules (stock valuation, VAT treatment, payroll calculations) in plain language, write test cases from real transactions, and review each release against them. Your team keeps building; you get an independent check that what they build is correct. More in Built your ERP in-house with AI?

Bangladesh compliance is often where it breaks

Generic implementations often stop short of local rules, and that's where Bangladeshi projects quietly fail: VAT prepared in Excel because the ERP's tax setup was never finished, payroll recalculated by hand because wage-board grades are hard-coded, LC and landed costs posted as one lump sum. These are no longer optional. In September 2026 the NBR made online return filing through the eVAT system mandatory for a list of manufacturers and service providers, including cement, tiles, medicines, paints, beverages, banking and insurance (The Financial Express). When the ERP doesn't produce the VAT figures, filing becomes a monthly reconciliation project.

Every rescue audit includes a compliance pass. The reference material is on this site: Mushak 6.3 in Odoo, NBR VAT compliance, wage-board payroll, and the dated Compliance Watch log.

Frequently asked questions

Can a failing Odoo implementation be saved, or do we have to start again?

Most can be saved. Odoo itself is rarely the problem; the usual causes are processes that were never mapped, dirty master data, too much customisation and users who were never properly trained. Those are fixable without throwing away the system. A restart only makes sense when the database is corrupted beyond repair, the customisations block every upgrade, or the wrong edition or version was chosen. The audit tells you which situation you are in before you spend money on either path.

Our implementation partner has left. Can you take over mid-project?

Yes. Taking over starts with an inventory: what is configured, what is customised, what data is in the system, what was promised and what was signed off. From there I write a short recovery plan with the decisions that need the owner's sign-off. Where custom code needs fixing, I specify and test the fix and work with your developers or a vetted technical partner.

We built our own ERP in-house. Do you work on non-Odoo systems?

Yes, at the process, data and adoption level. I don't rewrite your code; I act as the functional architect and QA partner for your developers, so they know exactly what to fix and in what order.

How much does an Odoo rescue cost?

The health check is free. The audit is a fixed fee agreed upfront, based on the number of companies, modules and users, so an owner can approve it without a committee. The fix sprint is quoted as a fixed scope from the audit's own findings, so you know the full cost before committing.
